## THE SPINE (memorize this first — six beats, the whole deck)

1. **A gap exists** because ADR creation needs consent the Company hasn't given — supply cannot answer demand.
2. **It doesn't converge** — floor is a holder right (7bp), ceiling is somebody's decision. It *persists*, which is what makes it holdable.
3. **The desk manufactures the exposure** — six absorbed legs, one ticket, cross-margined.
4. **The financing subsidizes the wait** — rate differential pays most of the borrow; carry sits under breakeven everywhere except slow-and-expensive.
5. **The risk is the path, not the level** — gain capped, loss uncapped, borrow tightens exactly when the call arrives. So: sized, not stopped.
6. **Three archetypes, three expressions** — linear pair / long-local TRS / standby structure. The pitch ends in a mandate match, not a conviction.

Bridge phrase between any two beats if you blank: *"…and that's why the next question is [next beat]."*

- CLAIM: Market says "quota exhausted, might refill." Filings say: discretionary consent, no numeric cap anywhere in any SEC filing.
- WHY IT MATTERS: A quota has a knowable end; a discretionary gate does not → persistence *and* danger from the same clause.

- BRIDGE: "If the gap can't be arbitraged, what can you do with it? Hold it."
- INTERNALIZE: You read the F-6; the market read a headline. That's the whole edge — say it with the documents, not with adjectives.

### S2 — Why the premium exists (the honest thesis)

- THREE-WORD FRAME: durable (consent) / attractive (56% beat carry at low borrow over 21.6y) / timely (funding leg cheapens if Fed hikes).
- BRIDGE: "You can't reach it by converting. You reach it by holding the spread — so how do we build the hold?"

### S7 — Three legs / FX channel
- CLAIM: Won moves the premium ~1.12pt per 1% (real) but it's 1.3% of daily variance — a channel, not a signal. Tested twice, p=0.25, effect *shrank* as sample grew.
- INTERNALIZE: You report the null with the same confidence as a positive. This slide is a credibility deposit, spend 30 seconds max.
- BRIDGE: "Two terms in the P&L. One is known. Here's what the unknown one can do."

### S8/S9 — Scenario PnL
- THE IDENTITY: P&L = Δπ·N − C·t. Δπ has zero drift (no convergence force — the gate is why). C accrues no matter what.
- THE PICTURE: black line = carry the trade can bear at each half-life (105/82/67 at 211d/302d/391d); band = what it costs (7–70). Clears everywhere except the one corner.
- ROM ASYMMETRY (the unit a book runs on): full compression ≈ +0.46× margin; the realized first-week widening ≈ −1.68× — *the best path pays a fraction of margin, the realized one cost more than all of it.*
- THE EPISTEMIC FLEX: "No row carries a probability, deliberately — this repository has no distribution over terminal moves and does not manufacture one." Deliver flat; it lands harder.
- BRIDGE: "Which brings us to the section that exists because of that −1.68."

### S10 — Stress hurts
- CLAIM: The realized 16%→52% replayed = peak call ~43% of notional cross-margined (73% of two-ticket).
- NETTING HONESTY: netting removes about a quarter of the call, *not most of it*, because both legs move together in exactly the episode that produces the call. Say this unprompted — it's the most trustworthy sentence in the deck.
- WRONG-WAY: borrow tightens as premium widens → the call and the recall arrive together.
- BRIDGE: "So how do you hold something like this? Not with stops."

### S11/S12 — Skew and stops
- SKEW: gain bounded by the 7bp floor; loss has no ceiling *on file* — the ceiling is a corporate decision. Negatively skewed **by construction, not by bad luck.**
- STOPS FAIL BOTH WAYS: 10pp stop fires on 54% of entries (and 22% of eventual winners); any stop loose enough to spare winners (>30pp) no longer bounds loss; SKHY's own 35.6pp in 3 sessions beats every stop tested AND the 21.6-year worst (25.3pp).
- THEREFORE: risk is budgeted by **size**, exit paths agreed **at entry** (Appendix 7 tree).
- BRIDGE: "Sized to what budget? That's a client-by-client answer — which is the client section."

### S13/S14 — Clients and execution
- CLIENT SPLIT: long-only holders (financing/borrow against existing position — small ticket, easy conversation) vs. platforms (standby structure, sized so the excursion sits *inside* their limit).
- VOL IDENTITY: the premium runs 212% annualized — triple KOSPI, 10× USD/KRW — idiosyncratic and Korean, not a global-risk bet.
- LIQUIDITY: $1bn ≈ 1.2 sessions at 10% ADV normally; in stress the *range* gaps 10×, volume doesn't → crossing cost explodes → another reason for size-not-stops.
- BRIDGE: "Which expression fits which mandate — that's the close."

## FAQ BANK

### Sales-side (expect most of these — they're selling comprehension, coverage, and safety)

**Q1. Give me the one-sentence version I can say on the phone.**
- "SK Hynix's US line trades ~30% over the identical Korean shares because new US shares need the company's consent; nobody can arb it away, and we can build you the position — financed, hedged, one ticket — where the rate differential pays most of the cost of holding it."
- Never add "and it should converge." The moment you promise convergence, compliance owns your afternoon.

**Q2. Who do I call first?**
- Three lists, three products: (1) **holders of the local line** (DART 5% filers, long-only/sovereign) → financing and hedging against what they already own — shortest conversation, no skew argument; (2) **structural-discount specialists** → the linear pair with borrow term and recall protection up front; (3) **platforms/pods** → the standby structure only — their drawdown limits cannot hold the naked pair and we say so first, because that's how you keep the relationship.
- Do NOT open any call with the linear pair unless the mandate can hold a discount without a date.

**Q3. What does the desk actually earn?**
- Financing spread both legs, borrow spread, FX, execution — paid across every scenario. Carry-agnostic revenue is *why* we can be honest about direction.
- If a client asks the same: same answer. Transparency here is the sale.

**Q4. What's the client's downside — how do I say it without killing the trade?**
- Say it exactly like the deck: gain is capped by a 7bp floor; loss has no ceiling *on file* because the ceiling is the company's decision. Then immediately: "which is why we size to a drawdown budget instead of pretending a stop works, and why two of our three expressions don't carry the naked short at all."
- The wrong move is softening the skew. The client's risk officer will find it in one read, and then you've lost both of them.

**Q5. Why hasn't this closed already, if everyone can see it?**
- Because the mechanism that closes gaps like this — create new ADRs, sell them into the premium — is shut by consent. Everyone CAN see it; nobody can act on it in the closing direction. Seeing ≠ arbitraging is the entire product.

**Q6. Why now?**
- Entry is at the wide end of a family that sustains double-digit premia for decades; the funding tailwind exists today (72bp/yr, grows if the Fed hikes); and the 2× ETF suspension is a name-specific flow event. But: "wide" is a level observation, not a timing call — we do not forecast the gate. If the client needs it resolved by a date, this is not their trade (it's a mandate gate, literally gate #6).

**Q7. Didn't this thing just blow up? I heard the premium doubled.**
- Yes — 16→52% in 13 sessions, and it's ON the risk slides, replayed as a margin path. That excursion is why the deck leads risk with sizing, why netting exists, and why the standby structure exists. "The worst week is our exhibit, not our footnote" — say that sentence; it converts fear into credibility.

**Q8. What if the client only wants the Korean leg / the cheap side?**
- Perfect — that's the long-local TRS expression: the compression view, no borrow, no short, no wrong-way risk. Desk earns swap financing. Smallest product, cleanest conversation, real revenue. Never force the pair on a level-view client.

**Q9. How fast can they get out?**
- Normal: $1bn in ~1.2 sessions at 10% ADV — exit is genuinely easy. Stressed: the range gaps 10× while volume doesn't, so crossing gets expensive precisely when they want out. Honest version: "getting out is cheap on any day except the day you'll most want to." Then point at sizing.

**Q10. What's onboarding friction?**
- KRX investment registration + standing proxy, ISDA capable of a bespoke TRS, margin docs. It has a clock — set expectations before the client falls in love. (Gate #3 exists because long-onlys without swap docs drop out here.)

**Q11. Client says: "this is LTCM / Royal Dutch Shell."**
- Agree first: same family, and we cite it — level view correct, interim path fatal. Then the three differences: sized to the documented excursion distribution (not stopped), financing termed (not rolled at the market's mercy), and for mandates that can't hold the path we pitch the structures that don't carry it. "The lesson of 1998 is that the path is the product — this deck prices the path."

**Q12. Can I send the client this deck?**
- No. This is the internal version — manager screens, rate-card internals, research annexes. Client version is a separate cut (and the manager pages never travel: rule-determined or not, you don't show a client a ranking of themselves and their competitors).

**Q13. Is this research or a trade idea? (classification)**
- Desk commentary / trade structuring — not published research, despite the template. Route any client-facing cut through compliance for the right disclaimers before anything leaves the building.

**Q14. What happens if the Fed cuts?**
- The funding leg is long the front end: a 25bp cut *adds* ~2bp/mo to client carry; a hike takes the same away — but the tailwind headline is gross. Net of our card, the differential is partly consumed; Appendix 0 has the split. Don't quote 72bp to a treasurer without the net number in the same breath.

**Q15. Who eats a borrow recall?**
- Structurally the short leg's holder — which is why term borrow with recall protection is priced *at entry* for the linear pair, and why "sourced cheap or willing to drop the short leg entirely" is a mandate gate. If we can't term it, we say so before the ticket, not after the recall.

### Trader/PM-side (fewer, sharper)

**T1. Your entry is 22.6 on one slide and 31.44 on another.**
- "31.44 is current (7/29, primary-official close); 22.6 was the mid-July anchor — the premium widened nine points during the build, which is the widening scenario realized live. The deck should carry one dated number and will." Concede, date, move on. Never improvise a third number.

**T2. 28bp or 18bp — which carry is real?**
- 28 = gross, no card, mid-special (stale slide). 18 = carded at the tight special; 70 at the wide. The split-card decomposition (App 0): non-borrow subtotal +5.0, special decides the rest. The honest headline: at crisis card + dearest special it's 90 vs 83 — negative headroom.

**T3. Where's the actual borrow quote?**
- Bracketed, flagged "desk quote pending" on every artifact — deliberately. First deliverable after this meeting is the SBL term indication; the model reprints on one config edit. A pitch that fakes a quote is worse than one that brackets it.

**T4. Why not model it as OU / where's your half-life conviction?**
- OU is misspecified — the correct null is one-sided reflection with state-dependent upper drift; there's no restoring force to estimate. The 302d "half-life" is a conditional decay estimate with a 211–391 interval, used only to bound bearable carry — never as a forecast.

**T5. Why is short-ADR the primary expression if elevated entries lose at mid carry?**
- Primary *for one archetype* whose mandate holds discounts without dates; within the pair, compressions historically close through the US leg falling. For everyone else the primary expression is a different product — that's the client section's whole point.

**T6. You hedged FX — am I currency-neutral?**
- No: ~18% of the US leg stays exposed after hedging the Korean leg — near the widest this comparison pair has shown. It's disclosed, not solved.

**R2. The unbounded-loss language — deliberate?**
